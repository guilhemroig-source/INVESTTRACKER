WHAT OGE FORM 278-T SAYS

How to read these filings

What is disclosed.

A periodic transaction report lists purchases, sales and exchanges of securities above $1,000 made by or on behalf of the filer, the filer's spouse, or a dependent child.

Not who directed it.

The form names the asset, the date and the band. It does not say whose account the trade sat in or who decided to make it, so nothing on this page attributes a trade to a person.

A band, not an amount.

Each transaction is disclosed in a range such as $15,001 to $50,000. The band is the filing, and we do not narrow it.

The filing deadline.

A periodic transaction report is due within 30 days of the filer being notified of the transaction and no later than 45 days after it took place. The days-to-file column counts calendar days from the trade date to the filing date.

Bonds, notes and funds are in the record.

Municipal bonds, corporate notes and funds appear in the filings alongside stocks (417 of the 7,514 rows on record). They are shown with their own type.

A disclosure is a record of a transaction, not evidence of anything else, and nothing here is investment advice. Sourcing and the limits of this data are on the methodology page.

How long after a trade is it reported?

Across the record the median gap between a trade date and its filing date is 60 days, and 86.1% of the transactions were filed more than 45 days after the trade. A periodic transaction report is due within 30 days of the filer being notified of the transaction and no later than 45 days after it took place.

Does the report say who made each trade?

No. OGE Form 278-T covers purchases, sales and exchanges above $1,000 made by or on behalf of the filer, the filer's spouse, or a dependent child, and it does not state who directed a given transaction or whether it was made by a manager under an arrangement. Amounts are disclosed as bands, not exact figures, and the record includes municipal bonds, corporate notes and funds alongside stocks.
